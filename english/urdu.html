📜 Urdu History — Brief

Urdu developed over centuries in South Asia through contact among speakers of local Indo-Aryan languages and Persian, Arabic and Turkic languages. Its literary and administrative traditions grew particularly under the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal-era cultural worlds, and later in the Deccan and North India.

The language has been known by several historical names and forms, including Hindavi/Hindustani in different contexts. By the 18th–19th centuries, a highly developed Urdu literary tradition emerged, especially in poetry. Mir Taqi Mir, Mirza Ghalib, Muhammad Iqbal and many other writers became important figures in its literature.

Today Urdu is an important language of South Asia. It is an official language of Pakistan and has recognised status in several parts of India. Urdu and Hindi share a large everyday vocabulary and a closely related grammatical base, while their standard written forms use different scripts and have different literary vocabularies.

🔤 Urdu Alphabet

Urdu uses a modified Perso-Arabic script. Letter shapes can change depending on whether a letter occurs at the beginning, middle or end of a word.

Important joining idea

Some letters do not connect to the letter after them. Common examples include:

ا د ڈ ذ ر ڑ ز ژ و

Do not try to memorise every positional form on day one. First recognise the letter and sound; then practise joining.

Gender

Urdu nouns are commonly treated as masculine or feminine. Agreement can affect adjectives and verbs.

اچھا لڑکا
achchha larka — good boy
اچھی لڑکی
achchhi larki — good girl
Present tense pattern

میں + verb + ہوں

میں پڑھتا ہوں۔

Main parhta hoon — I study/read. A feminine speaker commonly uses پڑھتی.

Past and future — starting point
میں گیا۔

Main gaya — I went.

میں جاؤں گا۔

Main jaaunga — I will go. Gender and agreement create additional forms.

📖 Reading Practice

میرا نام احمد ہے۔ میں ممبئی میں رہتا ہوں۔ مجھے اردو پڑھنا اور سیکھنا پسند ہے۔ میں ہر روز تھوڑی دیر اردو کی مشق کرتا ہوں۔

Roman: Mera naam Ahmad hai. Main Mumbai mein rehta hoon. Mujhe Urdu parhna aur seekhna pasand hai. Main har roz thori der Urdu ki mashq karta hoon.

Meaning: My name is Ahmad. I live in Mumbai. I like reading and learning Urdu. I practise Urdu for a little while every day.

Reading technique

  1. Look at the whole word.
  2. Identify familiar letters.
  3. Read from right to left.
  4. Use the Roman line only after trying the Urdu.
  5. Read the same passage aloud three times.

🎓 Advanced Urdu

Formal vocabulary

Idioms

آنکھوں کا تارا

“Apple of one's eye” — a very dear person.

Proverb

جیسی کرنی ویسی بھرنی

As you act, so shall you receive.

Literary Urdu

محبت، خیال، زندگی، آزادی

love, thought, life, freedom

Advanced study path

  1. Master verb forms and agreement.
  2. Study compound verbs such as کر دینا and لے لینا.
  3. Learn formal vocabulary from news and essays.
  4. Read short stories and essays.
  5. Progress to poetry with a glossary.
